Christmas Coloring Pages: 2 Free Bold & Easy Printables

Coloring pages come in two flavors: the intricate kind with a hundred tiny details, and the bold-and-easy kind with thick outlines and big open spaces. These two free Christmas coloring pages — a decorated tree and a cheerful snowman — are firmly the second kind. They're designed for kids, for beginners, and for anyone who wants relaxing holiday coloring without squinting at fiddly details or going outside the lines every three seconds.

What "bold and easy" means

Bold-and-easy coloring pages share three traits. Thick outlines give the crayon a clear border to stay inside, which is exactly what young colorers and shaky hands need. Big simple shapes — a triangle tree, circle ornaments, a round snowman — mean large areas to fill with satisfying strokes of color instead of fiddly corners. No shading or fine detail keeps the page about choosing colors and having fun, not about precision.

This style works across ages for different reasons. For toddlers and preschoolers, the thick lines forgive wobbly control and the big spaces match their big arm movements. For older kids, the simple designs are a blank canvas for creativity — patterns on the ornaments, a sunset sky behind the snowman, glitter glue on the star. For adults, bold-and-easy pages are genuinely relaxing: low decisions, high satisfaction, and a finished page in twenty minutes instead of three evenings.

Ways to use these pages beyond coloring

A printed coloring page is a craft supply, not just an activity. Here are a few ideas that turn these two free pages into holiday projects:

  • Homemade cards: color the tree, cut it out, and glue it to folded cardstock. A handmade card from a grandchild beats a store-bought one every time.
  • Gift tags: color the snowman, cut it into a tag shape, punch a hole, and write the name on the back.
  • Window decorations: color with markers, then tape the pages to a window — the light shining through looks like stained glass.
  • Coloring contest: print a stack for a holiday party and let everyone vote on categories: most colorful, most creative, best use of glitter.
  • Advent countdown: print one page per day in December and work through them as a family ritual.

Printing tips

Print at 100% scale (not "fit to page") on regular US Letter paper so the outlines stay crisp. Standard copy paper works fine for crayons and colored pencils; if you're using markers, print on slightly heavier paper or cardstock so the ink doesn't bleed through. And print extras — the best coloring sessions are the ones where nobody has to wait for a turn or worry about making a mistake, because there's always another copy.
